# Before writing: agreement and checks

## Purpose

Use this guide when a writing task will involve multiple discussions, alternatives, older drafts, or repeated edits. Send the agreement below at the start, then update the effective-state record after significant decisions.

## Starting agreement

```text
For this task, distinguish the discussion from the final artifact.

We may propose, compare, and reject alternatives during discussion. Discussion alone does not make an idea eligible for the final draft. Only explicitly approved content belongs to the effective state.

Organize task information into four categories:
1. Approved content: facts, claims, definitions, wording, and structure eligible for the final draft.
2. Approved changes: confirmed edits to apply.
3. Internal rationale and excluded history: decision reasons, rejected options, old wording, and superseded decisions that must stay out of the draft.
4. Unresolved matters: open questions; do not silently select an outcome that would materially change the result.

Later explicit decisions supersede earlier ones. Use rejected or superseded material only for auditing. Do not reintroduce it through denials, comparisons, disclaimers, or explanatory asides.

Before final drafting, extract a clean execution list containing only effective content and approved changes. Do not retell rejected alternatives or explain why an older option was not chosen.

State the effective position directly. Use phrases such as “not X but Y”, “no longer”, “unlike the old approach”, or “to avoid misunderstanding” only when the intended reader needs that comparison, history, or exclusion.

Until I request final drafting or revision, discuss the task and record decisions without rewriting the final artifact.
```

## Before final drafting

- [ ] Approved content and approved changes are recorded explicitly.
- [ ] Each material resolved issue has one current effective decision.
- [ ] New decisions supersede and identify older decisions.
- [ ] Rejected alternatives, old wording, and internal reasons are in the excluded area.
- [ ] Open matters have not become approved claims.
- [ ] Decision explanations are distinguished from final wording.
- [ ] The audience's need for comparisons, rebuttals, or historical context is clear.
- [ ] A clean execution list has been extracted from the effective state.
- [ ] That list does not retell discarded versions.

## Before delivery

- [ ] Substantive statements trace to approved content or designated sources.
- [ ] Superseded definitions, numbers, conclusions, and approaches are absent.
- [ ] The draft does not keep defending a deleted option.
- [ ] Internal revision reasons have not become audience-facing prose.
- [ ] Denials and comparisons serve the reader.
- [ ] Title, body, abstract, examples, tables, and figures share the same effective state.

## Short prompt

```text
Keep discussion separate from the final artifact. Draft only from explicitly approved content. Use rejected ideas, older versions, internal revision reasons, and superseded decisions only for auditing; do not reintroduce them through “no longer”, “not X but Y”, or references to an old approach. Extract a clean execution list before drafting, then audit for decision-history leakage. Preserve comparisons and qualifications that the intended reader actually needs.
```
